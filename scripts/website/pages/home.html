A Decentralized Agentic IDE

One desktop app for the coding agents you already use — {{harness:claude_code}}, {{harness:codex}}, {{harness:copilot}}, {{harness:opencode}} and more: a Project IDE where they work beside you, agents and teams to staff the work, workflows you read before they run, and goals you come back to.

No server and no account: your workspace lives on your machine, your identity is a Nostr key, and the people you invite meet you over relays that carry only ciphertext.

What Bisa does with each

01Inbox

Everything that needs you, in one place

You decide; you don't drive. Anything that waits on a person waits here — and nowhere else.

  • A row is a thing — a goal, a channel, a conversation, a workstream, a project, a workflow — carrying what is asked of you and what happened.
  • A gate to approve or decline, a question with the answers the agent thinks it has, a held step to release.
  • Say I'm not sure, and the agent comes back with a narrower question.
  • A harness waiting at its prompt in a terminal is a row too, with Open the terminal.
{{figure:inbox}}

02Agents

Agents that sign their own work

An agent is a definition — a prompt, a harness, a model plan with failover, an effort, skills and MCP servers — with a keypair of its own, so its work is signed as itself. Bisa ships no harness: it drives the ones on your machine.

core

The General Agent

Answers what is said to nobody in particular, and staffs the work: it installs from the catalog the agents and teams a goal needs, and assigns them.

core

The Workflow Agent

Designs and repairs workflows. It asks what it needs to know and proposes — and never adopts, starts or changes one behind your back.

core

The Decision-Making Agent

Judges where a decision point asks it, with a confidence. It holds no conversation, signs nothing, and stays off until you choose who judges.

{{count:agents}} agents in the catalog, named after jobs you know

Installed on demand — never seeded — each with the skills its job needs. A fresh workspace holds the General Agent, the Workflow Agent and the general channel; the rest arrives when a goal asks for it, or when you do.

  • Models with failover: fallback, weighted, round-robin, least-busy or auto-route, with live health on each.
  • Effort, fitted: six levels from minimal to max, each fitted to what the harness and the model take.
  • Your MCP servers, probed for health, their tools judged like commands.

03Teams

Teams: agents and people, staffed together

A team gathers agents — and people — under a purpose. Assign a goal to a team and the work is staffed from it.

  • One level deep: a team holds agents and people, never another team.
  • Its agents take the work, and the people on it may decide the goal's gates.
  • {{count:teams}} teams in the catalog, from discovery to launch — each installed with the agents it brings, and each agent with its skills.
  • Stand a team down and it takes no work; it comes back as it was.

04Projects

A full IDE, where agents work beside you — never on top of you

A project is a real folder — created, cloned, imported or adopted — and Bisa never writes into one it did not create. Every piece of work gets a workstream: a branch and a checkout of its own. Switching never touches your tree, and nothing in it can be made unrecoverable.

Git history

  • A streaming commit graph, staging by hunk or by line, interactive rebase without a terminal.

05Workflows

Work as a graph you can read before it runs

A workflow is how a piece of work runs: a small graph of steps — agents work, checks judge, people answer and approve, the world is waited for. You see every step before anything starts, and you watch each one while it runs.

  1. A graph of steps

    {{count:step-kinds}} types of step in four families: events — what happens, gateways — what routes, loops — what repeats, tasks — what works. Conditions are a closed, typed set: there is no expression language to script around.

  2. Made three ways

    Drawn by hand in the designer, installed from one of {{count:templates}} templates, or proposed by the Workflow Agent — from the closest template — and adopted by you.

  3. Run, watched, kept

    A run carries the workflow — on a goal, or alone in the workspace. Each step wears its state on the canvas, what needs a person waits in the Inbox, and every fact is a signed event in the run's journal.

The designer

Where a workflow is drawn and read — here, the catalog's {{template-name:bug-fix}}: reproduce a defect, fix it, prove the fix, review it and ship it, with a way back to the reporter when the defect cannot be reproduced.

{{figure:workflow-designer}}

  1. The palette

    Every step, in its four families — events, gateways, loops and tasks. Drag one onto the canvas where it belongs, or click it to add.

  2. The canvas

    The graph, read top to bottom: a branch for each answer, a way back when a check fails. Tidy lays it out again.

  3. The properties

    Of the workflow, or of the step you select: name, description, tags and inputs — and its problems, said before anything runs.

  4. Three panes

    Properties, Agent and Runs, on the rail at the right edge; above it all, the revision, whether it runs, and what uses it.

  • Every save a revision; when someone else saved first, keep yours on top of theirs or take theirs.
  • Turn it On, and the events it listens for begin its runs.

The Workflow Agent, beside the canvas

Ask it in the Agent pane, in your own words — to draft a workflow, add a step, change a branch or explain the graph. It saves the workflow, and the canvas follows while you watch.

  • It says what it built and why, step by step — and what it could add next, such as a schedule.
  • Each workflow keeps its own conversations, beside its canvas.
  • What it saves is a new revision of the workflow, like any save of yours.

06Goals

Say what you want. Come back to it.

A goal is a stated want, the workflow that makes it real, the run that carries it, and a signed journal of everything that happened. You choose who designs and who adopts.

The three modes of a goal
ModeWho designsWho adopts and runs
auto (the default)the Workflow Agent, with a default for every inputthe platform adopts, starts and repairs it, and asks you after three failed repairs — or where only a person can act
guidedthe Workflow Agent proposesyou: Adopt and start, Request changes, Edit the steps or Decline
manualyou, on a blank canvasyou; the agent drafts only when asked

An example, in guided mode

“{{example-goal:mobile-app}}”

  1. you

    You say it

    In the app's New goal, in one sentence. The goal is captured; nothing runs yet.

  2. design

    The Workflow Agent proposes

    It starts from the closest template — {{template-name:mobile-release}} covers the build-to-store half — asks you first if it needs to, and proposes the steps.

  3. you

    You adopt it

    The proposal waits in your Inbox. Adopt it, ask for changes, or edit the steps yourself. The General Agent staffs it: the Researcher, the UX Designer, the Mobile Developer and the Code Reviewer are installed from the catalog.

  4. agents

    Agents work — and ask you what is yours

    The research waits for your pick; the screens are drawn; the app is built in a workstream of its own and run on the simulators; tests and review loop until it is shippable.

  5. you

    You approve the submission

    The gate is in your Inbox. Nothing leaves for a store before your yes.

  6. with you

    Guided through both stores

    The Mobile Developer walks each store's listing and submission with you: you sign in on the embedded browser's tab, and the guard asks you before each submission goes.

  7. finished

    Published

    The run ends. The goal's journal keeps every step, signed — and a goal can listen, to run the next release on a schedule.

The Pulse is the workspace's live feed — every step that moves, every message, every agent at work — so you can look in without asking anyone.

  • A new fact arrives within a second, and the feed pages back as far as the workspace goes.

08Channels

People and agents, in the same rooms

  • Standing rooms with rosters — a directory, not a subscription: nobody answers unless addressed.
  • A message that names nobody reaches the General Agent, which answers or hands it to the agent that should.
  • Replies stream as they are written, the agent's thinking folded above them; react, reply, retract.
  • Conversations about any goal, workflow, project, note or drawing; what an agent makes — a page, a chart, a sheet, a deck, a PDF — renders live in the conversation.

09Direct messages

One to one — with a person, or an agent

  • Talk to any agent directly, on its own harness and its own key.
  • With a person on another machine, a direct message is encrypted pairwise to the two of you and to nobody else.
  • A message from outside is read by the classifier before anyone hears it.

Judgements that know how sure they are

The Decision-Making Agent answers a typed question — is it so, which one, how much — with a calibrated number instead of a guess written into a rule. It is off until you choose who judges.

  • {{count:decision-points}} decision points ask it first — which model leads a launch, how hard it works, who of a pool takes a work item, whether a tool call is harmful — and fall back to their own rule the moment it is not sure.
  • Calibrated answers come from Jev or any RLCD model; a harness can answer too, marked uncalibrated.
  • A judge step routes a workflow by what it answers. A judgement never signs a gate.

Notes

A markdown note about the workspace, a project, a goal, a workflow, a channel or this machine — opened over whatever you are looking at, and kept in its place while you move around.

  • Write, Split and Read views; it saves as you stop typing, with find and replace.
  • Ask an agent from the note: it reads the note first, and writes into it only when you ask — and then only adds.
  • Your notes are a git repository of their own, and stay on this machine unless you push.

Drawing

An Excalidraw canvas over whatever you are looking at — a system's parts, a flow, a board, a map — with ten templates and the platform's own shape libraries.

  • Ask an agent and watch it draw: boxes, arrows bound between them, frames — a Mermaid flowchart laid out into real shapes.
  • A drawing travels to every member, and any member's agent can read it and draw into it.

Questions

What makes it decentralized?

There is no Bisa server. Your workspace is a folder on your machine, your identity is a Nostr keypair, and every agent signs its work with a key of its own. When you invite people, your node and theirs exchange sealed facts over Nostr relays — transport that carries ciphertext and is never an authority. A workspace with no relay at all is complete.

Is Bisa free?

Yes — it is open source under the MIT licence. The harnesses you run and their plans and keys are yours, and cost what they cost.

Do I need an account?

No. A keypair minted on first run is your identity, and everything lives on your machine, in ~/.bisa.

Does my code leave my machine?

Your harness sends what it reads to its own model provider, exactly as it would in a terminal — with every secret the redactor recognises replaced by a placeholder first. Bisa itself runs no server and no analytics; it contacts only services you use or switch on, and the security page lists each one with the setting that turns it off.

Which coding agents does it work with?

{{harness:claude_code}}, {{harness:codex}}, {{harness:opencode}}, {{harness:copilot}}, {{harness:grok}}, {{harness:pi_rpc}}, {{harness:omp}}, any agent that speaks ACP, A2A agents and a harness of your own. The harnesses page says what Bisa does with each.

Is it a sandbox?

No, and it does not pretend to be. Its protections are best effort: secrets are redacted, a guarded harness's commands are judged before they run, and an observed one runs under its own sandbox. Workstreams are separate checkouts, not containers.

Which platforms?

Bisa is available for macOS {{macos}} or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel — a signed, notarized disk image. Linux and Windows are coming soon; today the desktop builds there from source, unsigned, without the menu bar icon or system notifications.

Does it phone home?

No. Bisa has no server to call and sends nothing about you anywhere. Its diagnostic log stays on your machine.

How do I update?

Download the next release. There is no automatic update, and a new release may not read an older workspace — there is no migration: move ~/.bisa aside and start fresh. The changelog says what changed.

Which languages does the app speak?

English today. Every sentence the app says lives in a translation catalog, so another language is a folder of translations.
