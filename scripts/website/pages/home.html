Everything that needs you, in one place

Agents do the work; anything that waits on a person waits here, and nowhere else.

  • Approve or decline a gate, answer a question from the options the agent offers, or release a held step.
  • Say I'm not sure, and the agent comes back with a narrower question.
  • A harness waiting at its prompt in a terminal shows up here too.

Staff a goal with a whole team at once

A full IDE where agents work beside you, never on top of you

Every piece of work gets a workstream, a branch and a checkout of its own, so switching never touches your tree.

  • An editor, terminals with your harnesses in tabs, git and pull requests, and a browser beside the code.
  • In manual mode, every edit an agent makes waits for your Keep or Undo.
  • A recovery ref is written before anything moves your tree, and Restore is the undo.

Read every step before anything runs

A workflow is a small graph of steps (agents work, checks judge, people approve) that you read before it starts and watch while it runs.

  • Draw it in the designer, start from one of {{count:templates}} templates, or ask the Workflow Agent to propose one.
  • The Workflow Agent proposes and repairs, and never adopts, starts or changes a workflow behind your back.
  • Turn it On, and it starts a run whenever something it listens for happens, such as a schedule or a hook.

How a workflow is made and run

Say what you want. Come back to it.

State a goal in one sentence: agents design and run the work, and you decide what only a person can.

  • Three modes: auto designs, adopts and runs it; guided brings you the proposal to adopt; manual opens a blank canvas.
  • A goal always says who holds it now: you, agents, the world or the design.
  • Every step is kept in the goal's signed journal, and a goal can listen, to run again on a schedule.

Questions

Is Bisa free?

Yes. It is open source under the MIT licence. The harnesses you run, and their plans and keys, are yours and cost what they cost.

Do I need an account?

No. A key made on first run is your identity, and everything lives on your machine, in ~/.bisa.

Does my code leave my machine?

Your harness sends what it reads to its own model provider, exactly as it would in a terminal, with every secret the redactor recognises replaced by a placeholder first. Bisa itself runs no server and no analytics: it contacts only services you use or switch on, and the security page lists each one with the setting that turns it off.

Is it a sandbox?

No, and it does not pretend to be. Its protections are best effort: secrets are redacted, a guarded harness's commands are judged before they run, and a harness Bisa only observes runs under its own sandbox. Workstreams are separate checkouts, not containers.

Which platforms?

macOS {{macos}} or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel, as a signed, notarized disk image. Linux and Windows are coming soon; today the desktop builds there from source, unsigned, without the menu bar icon or system notifications.

Start with Bisa?

The first launch checks five things (git, a harness and the three core agents) and installs nothing: where something is missing, it shows the official line to copy.
